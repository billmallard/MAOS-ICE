**Published PSRU failures (NTSB):**
- **RV-9 N362TS, 23 Aug 2009** (Eggenfellner Subaru per search context). NTSB probable cause: "Failure of the propeller speed reduction gearbox for undetermined reasons." Contributing were the pilot's decision "to disregard the manufacturer's mandate to replace the gearbox" (Gen-I to Gen-III) and "the manufacturer's failure to provide owners with technical substantiation". P21 [S: NTSB/ASN snippet. The PDF is encrypted and the ASN page returned 403.]
- **ERA14LA130**: "failure of the propeller spline shaft and PSRU input spline due to lack of inspection and lubrication ... lack of manufacturer guidance". The PSRU had not been inspected in about 325 h. P22 [S]
- **NYC95LA137**: a custom three-bearing PSRU failed because "thermal expansion of a mounting plate" bent the shaft. P22 [S]
- **CEN12LA470**: "spline-shaft and adapter gear wear" failure at 247 h in an auto-engine reduction gearbox. P22 [S]
- Engineering context: EPI says belt PSRU life is "typically limited to two or three hundred hours at the loads imposed by a piston engine". P23 [S of EPI]

## 3. Open-source ECUs

- **rusEFI** firmware is "**GNU version 3** license with the following additional terms: rusEFI#1 ... intended for off-road land and water vehicles ... rusEFI#3: rusEFI is **not approved for manned aircraft applications**." The repo is active (pushed 2026-10-21). P34 [V, license.txt]
  - Hardware is mixed. "Half of the universal rusEFI boards are open source hardware. Half are not." "For the majority of Hellen vehicle-specific boards we only publish complete schematics while not publishing gerbers and BOMs." Summary line: "PnP: mostly not." P35 [V]
  - The **uaEFI** board is open: schematics, PCB and gerbers are public (KiCad is the project's PCB tool). It has 6 injectors, 6 coils and 2 electronic throttle bodies (ETB). Price is about **$200** per a forum quote; official price not verified. P36 [V], P37 [S]
- **FOME** (fork, founded "at the start of 2023") ships the **same license.txt** as rusEFI, including the GPLv3 plus "not approved for manned aircraft" terms. It "shares a large amount of its core with the rusEFI project". P38 [V]
- **Speeduino** firmware is **GPL-2.0** in the LICENSE file (the README badge says GPLv3, which is inconsistent). Hardware is under "**CERN Open Hardware License v2 Strong**". "All new designs have been made in Kicad". The project claims "over 1000 installations". Prices: Dropbear V2 "$420" (forum, Oct 2024); UA4C about $187 (forum snippet). P39 [V], P40 [F], P41 [S]
- **Aviation users** of rusEFI, Speeduino or FOME: **not found** (no documented flying installations). One HBA post (2015) says Megasquirt "isn't geared towards aviation (no redundancy, and limited analog inputs)". P42 [F]
- **Dual-ECU / redundancy work**: one rusEFI forum thread (for the Finke desert race) proposes two units "in parallel ... simple manual failover", with "injectors and coils connected in parallel". The answer noted rusEFI "is only endorsed for off-road use". No upstream redundancy feature was found. P43 [S; forum returned HTTP 500]

**Blockers for a two-lane aircraft FADEC, derived from the sources above:**
1. The license's additional term #3 (rusEFI and FOME) disclaims manned aircraft use. Whether that term can be enforced under GPLv3 section 7 is a legal question I did not research.
2. There is no lane arbitration, cross-channel data link or health monitoring upstream, only an ad-hoc parallel wiring idea (P43). Compare the 912 iS lanes and the ULPower active standby.
3. There is no single-fault-tolerant power architecture. Compare 912 iS Gen A/B with backup switch (P25) and EFII Bus Manager (P30).
4. Many board designs are closed (rusEFI Hellen boards). Using only fully open boards narrows the choice to uaEFI/Proteus-class wire-in boards, which are single-channel automotive boards with no aviation environmental qualification (none found).
5. There is no aviation flight history (P42).

## 4. Openness, law and IP

- **Copyright does not cover methods or dimensions.** US Copyright Office Circular 33 excludes "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery, regardless of the form in which it is described, explained, illustrated, or embodied". For a technical drawing, "the registration extends only to the original expression ... not to the underlying idea, methods, or systems". So a project can publish its *own* drawings and procedures that use factory facts (torque values, dimensions). It cannot copy the text or figures of a factory service manual. P44 [V, primary]
- **OEM ECU firmware**: the DMCA §1201 exemption (37 CFR 201.40) permits circumvention "when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, repair, or lawful modification of a vehicle or vessel function". It covers personal automobiles, boats and similar, and grants no permission to *redistribute* OEM code. Applying it to an aircraft engine is not established (not found). A replacement open ECU avoids the issue. P45 [V, primary]
- **Liability for open hardware**: no legal authority was found. The forum consensus (P24) is that disclaimers do not shield contributors. Treat this as an open legal question.
- **Examples of open conversions**: **none found.** FlyCorvair sells "a multitude of Corvair teardown, operation and other manuals" (P46 [M]). Auto PSRU's, SDS and EFII are proprietary. EPI publishes engineering essays but not drawings. MakerPlane is an open-source *airframe/avionics* project (snippet only, P47 [S]). "OpenPSRU": not found.

## 5. Builder labor hours

- **Vendor or Kitplanes hour estimates for installing an automotive-conversion FWF kit: not found.**
- One forum snippet reports a "complete engine/firewall forward installation in 130 hours", excluding cowl prep. The engine type is unclear and the result is not verified. P48 [S/F]
