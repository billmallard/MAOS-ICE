Documented failures and mitigations:
- Rotax: overload clutch disengages the propeller on a prop strike; a dog-gear torsional shock absorber smooths power pulses; the 915 adds a torsion shaft (2-3 deg twist). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotax_912
- Eggenfellner Subaru, RV-9A, NTSB ERA14LA130: prop spline shaft, drive-disk adapter and PSRU input spline failed at 325 h; total loss of thrust. https://generalaviationnews.com/2016/02/09/mechanical-issue-brings-down-rv-9a/
- NTSB NYC95LA137: PSRU shaft failure after a manufacturer design change.
- EPI: damaging torsional loads at 240-320 Hz; warns against low-inertia flywheels and torsionally rigid couplings; a vendor relying on a centrifugal clutch suffered a fatal failure of its own product. https://epi-eng.com/propeller_reduction_technology/torsional_vibration_issues.htm
- Belted Air Power: backfire can shear crank mounting bolts (Kitplanes, above).

## 7. CVT and multi-speed propeller drives

- No flying CVT or multi-speed propeller drive behind a piston engine was found. A two-speed gearbox appears only as a turbine patent concept (US6524068). An EAA forum thread on a CVT PSRU is discussion only.
- Rotax 582 B/C/E gearboxes are single fixed ratios (2.0-4.0:1). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotax_582
- Paramotors use centrifugal clutches, not CVTs.
- Rubber-belt CVT efficiency at 75-150 kW: **not found.** Scooter-CVT literature reports design-dependent efficiency (Lee & Sung, https://trid.trb.org/View/483264; Chen & Sung, https://www.inderscience.com/info/inarticle.php?artid=5195). A "70-80 %" snippet could not be traced and is not used.
